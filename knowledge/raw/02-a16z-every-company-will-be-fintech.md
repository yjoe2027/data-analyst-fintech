# Every Company Will Be a Fintech Company

**Author:** Angela Strange  
**Date:** January 21, 2020  
**Source:** Andreessen Horowitz

## Overview

Strange presents the thesis that nearly every company will eventually derive significant revenue from financial services. She argues that "as a service" infrastructure models—similar to Amazon Web Services for computing—are transforming banking and enabling this shift.

## Key Problem: Current Banking Dissatisfaction

The financial services industry faces a legitimacy crisis. Only "28 percent of the millennial and Gen Z generations trust their banks" to be fair and honest. Traditional banks struggle with innovation due to legacy systems, extensive physical infrastructure, and massive regulatory compliance costs. At major institutions, "75 percent of IT budgets" go toward maintaining existing products rather than developing new ones.

## The Infrastructure Solution

Strange identifies an emerging ecosystem of "as a service" fintech companies addressing specific banking stack components:

**Data Integration:** Plaid consolidates thousands of disparate bank integrations into a single API, enabling applications like Earnin and Blend to launch without rebuilding infrastructure.

**Core Banking:** Synapse provides account management and payment systems "as a service," allowing companies like Mercury (startup banking) and Propel (serving low-income families) to focus on customer experience rather than infrastructure.

**Compliance & Fraud:** Comply Advantage and Sentilink tackle money laundering detection and synthetic identity fraud respectively, automating previously manual processes.

## Real-World Examples

Non-financial companies are entering banking:

- **Apple** launched a credit card despite being primarily a technology company
- **Uber and Lyft** offer financial services to drivers, improving retention and customer lifetime value
- **Shopify** and **Mindbody** derive nearly 50% of revenue from financial services

## Future Implications

Strange envisions democratized financial access: "everyone, no matter their socioeconomic demographic...will have access to affordable financial services." Nearly 2,000 fintech companies launched in 2019 alone, representing thousands of experiments building atop this new infrastructure layer.
