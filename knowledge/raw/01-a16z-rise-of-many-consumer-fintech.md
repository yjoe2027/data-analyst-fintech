# The Rise Of Many in Consumer Fintech

**By:** Anish Acharya, Sumeet Singh, and Alex Immerman  
**Date:** November 19, 2021

## Introduction

The authors argue that consumer fintech will not become a winner-take-all market. Instead, they predict "the rise of many consumer fintech banks," with large platforms serving broad audiences alongside specialized services targeting niche segments with distinct needs.

## How Traditional Banking Evolved

The article traces banking history from local, relationship-driven institutions to national players leveraging economies of scale. However, community banks and credit unions haven't disappeared. Rather, they've thrived by serving narrow vertical audiences with customized offerings. Examples include:

- **Silicon Valley Bank:** Specializes in tech founders with illiquid assets
- **First Entertainment Credit Union:** Serves child actors
- **Navy Federal Credit Union:** Offers military-specific benefits

## The Consumer Fintech Opportunity

Consumer fintechs are replicating this model but with a crucial advantage: the internet enables them to "aggregate a customer segment...with shared needs" across geographic boundaries.

The authors note that seemingly competitive subprime neobanks—Chime, Current, Cash App, and Earnin—actually succeed by targeting different demographic segments within that broader market.

## The Four Banking Functions

Banks typically handle four core services: savings, spending, investing, and lending. Most consumer fintechs innovate around one primary category while serving a specific vertical audience. This differentiation, combined with audience focus, defines their brand and competitive position.

## Market Structure

The authors project numerous $10B-$100B consumer fintech companies emerging, each serving distinct vertical audiences defined by age, ethnicity, religion, sexual orientation, credit history, and other factors. The article includes visual representations showing opportunities across multiple segments—from Hispanic Americans to Gen Z students—and demonstrates additional potential within sub-categories like investment-focused fintechs.

## Conclusion

Rather than monopolistic consolidation, consumer fintech will feature "many winners." Long-term differentiation will stem from "features that are purpose built for a specific community or audience," particularly as consumers increasingly recognize limitations in traditional banking's fee structures and pricing models.
